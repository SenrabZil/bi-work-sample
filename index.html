Picking up a pilot in progress, and finishing it: the M2i Platform from pilot to regular operation

Approach note for the Technical Senior Data Analyst assignment, EIT RawMaterials.

Liz Barnes, Special Dimension, September 2026.

Read as it opens, this is the two-page approach note from the proposal: same content, same commitments. The sections marked deeper hold the supporting material — the layer map, feed register, dashboard mock-ups, market analysis, the Fabric migration path and the appendices — and open on a click. Dashboard and simulator figures are illustrative, not M2i data. The report mock-up I would use with stakeholders is a companion page, and the Power BI work sample built behind it is in the repository under Releases.

What I finish

The pilot’s stated purpose: a validated data model, on a documented lineage, behind tiered security, with usage measured. The things regular operation needs and a pilot is allowed to lack.

What I don’t touch

Nothing partners see, until the replacement reconciles against what it replaces. No production change in the first four weeks except a documented emergency fix.

When

Weeks one to four reading and checking. Weeks five to eight the first measured deliverable. Then the six RFP areas monthly, paced to the pilot’s end on 31 December 2027, a year before the contract does.

My current understanding

The M2i Platform is live, and it is a pilot. A Power BI report opened in the browser behind an emailed login code, partner-only, free of charge until the end of 2027, with the stated purpose of validating the data model before regular operation. The two-year contract runs from November 2026; the pilot is scheduled to end on 31 December 2027, extendable in six-month steps. So the assignment spans the pilot’s close and whatever comes after it, and the job is not to keep a pilot running. It is to finish one: to leave a platform that can be operated, charged for and handed on. Picking that up mid-flight, the first risk is not that I fail to add something new. It is that I break something partners already rely on, or repeat work that was done and never written down. Everything below is built around that risk, and pointed at that end date.

What finished looks like

A pilot is allowed to have an unvalidated model, undocumented lineage, flat access and no usage figures. Regular operation is not. These are the exit criteria I would ask the Innovation Team to agree in month one and hold me to at the pilot’s end. Each names the evidence that shows it is met, because a criterion without evidence is a hope.

1. The data model is validated

Every headline figure on every page has been recomputed from source at least once and the comparison filed. Open breaks have a materiality rating and an owner.

Evidence: the reconciliation register, complete, with dates.

2. Lineage is documented end to end

Source to tile for every dataset, tier recorded, harmonisation steps for derived data written down so the original can be reached again.

Evidence: the lineage map, and a successor able to trace one tile to source unaided.

3. Security follows the licences

Internal EIT RM users and external partners separated as tenants, each with its own authentication and administration; row- and object-level security tested per tenant, partner tier and restricted-use dataset; export controls removed where the licence requires; attribution kept.

Evidence: a test log per tenant, tier and restricted dataset. ING, Amsterdam 2014: designed the role-based access model for the loan loss provisioning system, four roles, each with its own view of the same provisioning data and a separation between who prepares and who approves.

4. Usage is measured

The pilot exists to collect user experience. By its end, active users, pages viewed and time on page are on a tile, per partner tier, for at least six months.

Evidence: the Platform health page with real numbers instead of a dash.

5. Releases run without me

A release gate like the one on the Platform health page, used for at least two dataset releases by someone other than the contractor.

Evidence: two filed release records with the gate ticked by a named person.

6. A successor could start on Monday

A handover pack: inventory, lineage, the reconciliation register, the release process, the assumptions register, the list of things that only work because someone remembers.

Evidence: the pack, and one walkthrough with the person who will own it.

The pilot-to-regular-operation transition also carries a commercial question that is EIT’s, not mine: the terms say partners have no claim to a paid service after the pilot. Whatever they decide, criteria three and four are what make either answer possible.

When logic moves from Power BI into Fabric, the Power BI version stays in place as the reference. The Fabric result is recomputed and compared at row level. Only when the comparison is clean is the old version retired. Try it: the gate at the bottom will not open while a break exists.

Market analysis the platform should make possible

deeper

Not my view of the market; that is the Innovation Team’s job and the partners’. What the data analyst owes them is that four kinds of question can be answered from the model with the assumptions visible. Each needs a named dataset, and each appears below with the tier it draws on.

  • Price against concentration. Does a commodity’s price volatility rise with its supplier concentration? Needs the Pink Sheet series and the Comext HHI on one grain. Derived tier.
  • Demand scenarios by end use. What does EU lithium, nickel or cobalt demand look like in 2030 under stated assumptions? Needs a scenario table with every assumption as a row, not a number typed into DAX. Material intensity is one of those rows per cathode chemistry, because that is how battery demand on the platform is cut. Proprietary tier, because EIT owns the assumptions.
  • Growth rates that can be audited. EIT’s CAGR KPIs recomputed from the series with the base year and end year shown on the tile, so a partner can see why 2019 to 2025 differs from 2021 to 2025.
  • Value-chain coverage. Where the EU has extraction but not processing, or processing but not recycling, by commodity. Needs the CRMA Tracker joined to the value-chain master, with operating capacity held apart from announced capacity so that today and potential are never one number.

Rules I keep throughout

Reference implementation before migration

Nothing that replaces existing logic is accepted on my say-so. It is accepted on a clean comparison against the logic it replaces, with the comparison filed as evidence.

Cboe Clear Europe, 2024–25: Power Query reference built first; AI-generated Python rebuild validated against it figure by figure; the freed capacity went to eight further regulatory deliverables.
Every figure that matters is recomputed once, independently

A headline number that reaches a dashboard has been reproduced from source at least once by a route that does not share code with the production logic.

De Nederlandsche Bank statistical return: every automated figure checked against a hand-built reference before submission, then the validated logic handed to the quant team to productionise.
Documentation is written while doing, not afterwards

Each change to a model, pipeline or report carries a note of what changed, why, and how to reverse it. Handover then becomes collecting notes, not reconstructing history.

Sympower, 2025–26: the handover toolkit for my own successor in the Nordic BESS analyst role: inventory, lineage, and the list of things that only worked because I remembered them.
The data tier travels with the data

Proprietary, restricted-use or derived is recorded on every dataset from ingest to tile, so security, export and attribution rules are enforced by the model rather than remembered by the analyst. When EIT RM replaces or removes a source, as the terms allow, the lineage shows exactly which tiles change.

Cboe Clear Europe: a regulatory data warehouse view for Risk Oversight where every column carried its source and reporting obligation, so a change upstream could be traced to every affected return. ING, Amsterdam 2014: the role-based access model for the loan loss provisioning system, four roles over one dataset, each seeing only what its role needed.
Regulation becomes data before it becomes a dashboard

A benchmark, an export control or a reporting rule is entered as a dated, sourced row with a scope before any tile refers to it. Then the tile can be recomputed when the rule changes, and the analyst can show which rule produced which number.

Cboe Clear Europe: workshops that turned ESMA’s SFTR requirements into agreed field-level logic across risk, operations and IT, before any report was built on them.
Monthly planning names outputs, not hours

Each deliverable is agreed as an implemented thing with an acceptance check before the hours are spent, in line with the RFP’s output-based review of time sheets.

Sympower, 125-asset migration: cutover runbook with source and data readiness checks, Datahub and eSett monitoring, and operational validation as the acceptance step.

From Power BI to Fabric, in that order

deeper

The platform’s reports are Power BI today and the RFP asks for selected logic to move into Fabric. The order matters more than the tooling: first make the Power BI model look like a lakehouse, then migrate one layer at a time with the old version kept as the reference, and switch the semantic model last. Partners see no change until every hop has reconciled. Open each stage.

Rule: restructure before migrating, migrate before switching, and retire nothing until the comparison is clean.
1. Shape the Power BI model like a lakehousemonths one to two

No Fabric yet. In the existing Power Query, the transformations are arranged into three named groups: Bronze (raw extracts as they arrive, not loaded), Silver (typed and cleaned, not loaded), Gold (the fact and dimension tables the model reads). Each Gold query ends with a written grain and source note, every measure is defined once in one place, and every dataset carries its data tier. Nothing changes for partners; the report looks the same.

Why first: the migration then becomes a rename, not a redesign. The M code in each group is what the Dataflows Gen2 will run; the group names are the lakehouse layer names. The work sample built for this proposal is structured this way.
2. Write the migration registermonth two

Every transformation and measure listed with where it lives today, its Fabric target, whether it moves, and the comparison evidence that has to be filed before the old version is retired. Not everything moves. A weighted average that is recomputed on every visual belongs in a Gold column; time intelligence and anything that depends on the slicer context belongs in DAX and stays; a status derived from two columns is derived once, in Silver, not per report.

Moves into Fabric

  • Source pulls and raw landing (Bronze)
  • Type, clean, derive-once columns (Silver)
  • Allocation and precomputed ratios at fact grain (Gold)
  • Scheduled refresh and its run log
  • Access roles, with membership as groups rather than names

Stays in the semantic model

  • Time intelligence (trailing twelve months, year on year)
  • Measures that depend on the filter context (weighted HHI across groups)
  • The release-gate verdict: the checks are written by the pipeline, the verdict stays a measure
  • Report-level formatting and thresholds stated on the page
3. Migrate one hop at a timemonth three on, paced by the monthly plan

Bronze first: a Dataflow Gen2 lands the raw extracts into a lakehouse on a schedule, and runs unattended twice before it counts (release-gate check six). Then Silver, then Gold, each a dataflow that reads the layer below and writes the next. After every hop the new layer is compared, row for row, against the Power Query output it replaces, and the comparison is filed. The .pbix keeps reading its original sources until Gold matches; only then does it repoint.

What partners notice: nothing. The dashboards read the same figures from a different place. That is the test of a correct migration, not a feature of it.
4. Switch the semantic model lastwhen Gold has reconciled

A Direct Lake semantic model is built on the Gold tables with the same measures, the same roles and the same names, edited from Power BI Desktop. The import model and the Direct Lake model run side by side and are compared at row level, exactly as the reference-versus-rebuild exercise above. When the comparison is clean the reports are repointed and the import model is retired, with the comparison record kept as the evidence.

5. Then what Fabric makes possibleregular operation

Once the stack is in place the things the pilot lacked stop being projects. Usage telemetry lands in Bronze like any other feed and becomes the Platform health tiles (deliverable 4). The independent recompute that today is a Python script on someone’s laptop runs as a notebook against Silver, on a schedule, writing its result to the reconciliation register. A partner API, if EIT wants one, reads Gold tables that already carry their tier. And a successor who opens the workspace sees the same three layers, the same names, and the notes written while doing.

Appendix B: assumptions to confirm

deeper

Each of these is checked in week one. Where one turns out wrong, the plan changes; the rules do not.

  • The webinar of 17 September presented the platform areas as Trade, Production, Commodity and Sector; the terms of 13 May list Region, Trade, Commodity and Industry. Which naming is current, and whether the downloadable Region tables still sit in an area of their own, decides whether Region remains the first candidate for weeks five to eight.
  • The terms name the Battery dashboards in Industry as restricted-use content; the webinar announced a Battery dashboard for the last quarter of 2026. Whether that is a first release or a new version of existing pages is a week-one question. The licence obligations apply either way.
  • The Fabric backend follows a Bronze / Silver / Gold lakehouse layering. The RFP refers to Gold-layer structures, which implies it, but the actual layering is not published.
  • Usage data exists well enough to identify the five most-used pages. It may not: usage monitoring is itself a deliverable, in which case the first five are chosen with the Innovation Team.
  • The public feeds in the register are candidates, not confirmed sources. Which of them M2i already harmonises into its derived-data tier is not stated anywhere public.
  • The restricted-use Battery dashboards are fed by a commercial data provider under licence. The provider is not named; the T&Cs only define the tier.
  • The contractor works day to day with the Innovation Team and a Project Officer for Critical Raw Materials, on the strength of the two postings, rather than with a dedicated data-engineering team.
  • Reconciliation of derived data back to the public original is possible, meaning the harmonisation steps are recorded somewhere. If they are not, recording them becomes the first documentation deliverable.
  • The dashboard and refresh figures on this page are illustrative. The 20-minute refresh target, the commodity values and the CRMA shares are placeholders shaped to look like the real thing; none is a claim about M2i.
  • The geopolitical panel’s two index series are approximations shaped to published monthly levels, credited to Mirzak Analytics as analysis method, not as data. Replacing them with the LME Index (or S&P GSCI Industrial Metals) and GPR exports is a pre-publication step; the correlations on the page will change when it happens.
  • The demand simulator’s base of 2.4 million EU EV sales in 2025, the 65 kWh pack and 0.85 kg LCE per kWh are round illustrative starting values, not sourced figures.
  • The Trade, Region and Industry dashboard tiles use invented partner shares, HHI levels, import values and capacity figures shaped to be plausible; none is a published statistic.
  • The Microsoft DP-600 line is a commitment to sit the exam by 30 November 2026, not a claim to hold the certification now.

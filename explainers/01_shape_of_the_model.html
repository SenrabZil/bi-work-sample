03

Why the source register is the hub

19 of the 21 fact tables carry a dataset key, so every one of their figures points back to the source it came from. That single link does two jobs. It lets any tile say where its number came from, and it's where row-level security starts: a role filters the dataset table, and the filter flows out to every figure from a restricted source, such as licensed prices.

The two fact tables without a dataset key are operational: the refresh log and the rebuilt trade table used for migration checks. Three more tables stand alone with no relationships at all: the measure register, the release gate and the migration worklist.

04

Every wire points one way

All 53 relationships are many-to-one, single direction and active. A selection flows from a lookup table to the figures and never back, so there's no ambiguous path for a filter to take and no many-to-many join to double-count. There's one link between two lookup tables: commodity rolls up to commodity group, because some sources only report at group level.

Select Trade in the page list above and you'll see it: the trade page draws on five lookup tables, including commodity group.

The structure is the real model's, taken from its project files. Figures elsewhere in this series are illustrative.

05

The rule, in one breath

Figures in the middle, lookups around them, every wire one way, and every figure tied to its source.

A star schema with single-direction, many-to-one relationships is predictable to filter and cheap to secure. Tying 19 of 21 fact tables to one source register gives the report its provenance and its security in the same move.

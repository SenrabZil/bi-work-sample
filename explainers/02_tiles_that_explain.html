Every number
explains itself.

Each tile carries its figure, where it came from, and a short explanation. The design choice behind that is why the model has 219 measures, not 122.

with Elizabeth Cecilia. A number nobody can explain is a number nobody should act on.

01

Three things on every tile

The title carries the data tier in brackets, so the reader knows how far to trust the source before reading the figure. The subtitle is a basis line saying which dataset and year the number comes from and how it's calculated. The "i" button opens a short explanation of under 200 characters, with the full version kept in a register table.

None of that text is typed into the visuals. It lives in the model as measures, bound to the subtitle and to the button's tooltip, so the wording can be changed in one place and checked by a script.

02

One measure, or one per tile?

The report has 52 tiles, and each needs its own basis line and explanation. The obvious shortcut is one shared measure that looks up the right text for whichever tile it sits on.

03

Why the shortcut breaks

A subtitle or a button tooltip can only point at a single measure. It can't pass that measure a key saying "I am the reserves tile". The only way to tell a shared measure which tile it sits on is a filter on every visual, and filters are exactly what users and other developers clear, copy and change. When that happens nothing errors. The text just goes blank or wrong, quietly, on every tile at once.

One measure per tile costs more names in the field list but nothing at run time, because they hold text rather than calculations. They're generated from the register, so nobody types 97 measures by hand, and the wording can't drift between the tile, the tooltip and the documentation.

Tiles, figures and basis wording here are illustrative. The real text lives in the model's measure register.

04

Where 219 measures come from

Click a band to see what it holds.

Explanations One per tile, bound to the "i" button's tooltip. Under 200 characters each.

97 of the 219 are generated text measures. The other 122 are calculations: 47 core business measures and 75 added as the pages grew.

05

The rule, in one breath

Every tile's text is its own measure, generated from one register.

A shared measure looks tidier but depends on filters anyone can clear, and it fails silently. One measure per tile, per job can't be broken by accident, costs nothing to run, and keeps the tile, the tooltip and the documentation saying the same thing.
